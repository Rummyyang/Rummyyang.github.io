\begin{rSection}{ONGOING RESEARCH}
\textbf{\href{https://rummyyang.github.io/research/opd/opd_deck.html\#1}{How On-policy Distillation Affects Reasoning and Subsequent RL}}\hfill 2026.09 - 2026.10\\
The University of Hong Kong\quad |\quad Ph.D. Research\hfill \href{https://rummyyang.github.io/research/opd/opd_deck.html\#1}{[Slides]}
\\[1pt]Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B/4B students; 8B primary teacher
\\[1pt]{\small\textcolor{summarytext}{Accuracy figures below: Qwen3-1.7B, MATH-500, 16K generation budget, avg@4.}}
\begin{itemize}
\item Designed comparisons of teacher prompts, self-distillation, and direct RL to investigate why distillation can improve downstream RL results.
\item Switching the same teacher to a thinking prompt yielded 86.75\% accuracy for 1.7B, versus 73.45\% for the base model and 76.20\% for standard OPD; self-distillation at 1.7B and 4B achieved similar results.
\item Used budget curves, forced-finalization interventions, and stopping analyses to distinguish long pre-answer reasoning from continued generation after an answer, showing how correctness and timely delivery can diverge.
\item Under the same downstream RL setup, distillation followed by RL reached 87.25\% versus 81.70\% for direct RL, with gains at lower budgets accompanied by reduced accuracy at higher budgets.
\end{itemize}
\end{rSection}
